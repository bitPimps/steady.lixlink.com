Does a Relapse Reset Your Sobriety Date?

By the Steady team · Published November 27, 2026 · Updated November 27, 2026

Short answer: In most recovery traditions, yes: a relapse starts a new sobriety date and the counter goes back to zero. But the time you were sober doesn't disappear. Many people keep track of both their current streak and their longest one. Steady resets the clock when you log a slip and keeps your past streaks in your history.

Slip, lapse or relapse?

People use these words differently. Some call a single drink or one evening a slip or lapse, and a return to the old pattern a relapse. Others treat any use as a relapse. 12-step groups usually ask members to start a new sobriety date after any drink. Whatever word you use, the useful question is the same: what happened, and what will you do next?

Do you have to reset your counter?

It's your count, so it's your call, but an honest count is more useful. A tracker that matches reality shows you real patterns: how long your streaks tend to last, and when slips tend to happen. Resetting doesn't erase your progress either. Ninety sober days followed by a slip is still ninety days of practice, and your longest streak is still yours.

Relapse is common in recovery. The U.S. Surgeon General's 2016 report on addiction notes that recurrence rates for substance use disorders are no higher than those for other chronic illnesses such as diabetes, asthma and hypertension. A relapse is a sign to adjust the plan, not proof that it can't work.

What to do in the first 24 hours after a relapse

  1. Get safe. If you drank heavily for several days, stopping again can bring on withdrawal. Talk to a doctor, or call the SAMHSA National Helpline at 1-800-662-4357.
  2. Tell one person. A friend, sponsor, counselor or group. Shame grows in secret.
  3. Write down what happened. The time, where you were, who you were with, how you felt and what set it off. This is the most useful information you'll get.
  4. Reread your reasons. Your reasons for stopping haven't changed.
  5. Plan the next trigger. If it was a Friday evening, decide now what next Friday evening looks like.
  6. Start your new date. Day one again is still day one of something.

How Steady handles a slip

On the Today tab, tap Relapsed? Reset the clock under the check-in button and confirm. Steady logs the relapse and restarts your clock from that moment. Instead of a blank screen, it shows a short note with how long you were steady, your first reason, and links to your toolkit.

Your past check-ins aren't erased. With Premium, the history screen keeps every past streak, your longest streak and your steady days this year. Milestone notifications reschedule from your new start. If you'd rather wipe a habit's history completely, open its Edit screen and tap "Start over completely"; that deletes its check-ins and past streaks and can't be undone.

Steady's journal and (with Premium) Insights help with step 3: they show which triggers come up most and when cravings hit hardest, so your next plan is based on your own data.

Common questions

Does one drink reset my sobriety date?

In most 12-step traditions, yes. Outside those groups it's your choice, but an honest count shows you real patterns. Either way, the days you were sober still happened.

What's the difference between a slip and a relapse?

Many people use slip or lapse for a brief, one-off return to use, and relapse for going back to the old pattern. Definitions vary between groups and clinicians.

Do I lose my progress if I relapse?

No. Your sober days, what you learned and your longest streak remain. In Steady, past streaks stay in your history with Premium, and resetting the clock doesn't delete your check-ins.

How common is relapse?

Common. The U.S. Surgeon General's 2016 report says recurrence rates for substance use disorders are no higher than for other chronic illnesses such as diabetes, asthma and hypertension.
