Which Sobriety Apps Are Free?

By the Steady team · Published November 27, 2026 · Updated November 27, 2026

Short answer: Most sobriety apps are free to download, but few are completely free. They usually follow one of three models: free with ads, free with a paid subscription for extras, or free with a one-time unlock. Steady's core tracker is free with no ads and no time limit; a one-time $19.99 adds extras. Government tools such as NIAAA's Rethinking Drinking are free outright.

What "free" usually means for a sobriety app

"Free" on an app store means free to download. How the app makes money decides what you can actually use without paying. Here are the four models you'll run into:

ModelWhat you get freeWhat it costs you
Free with adsUsually everythingAds on screen, and sometimes data shared with ad networks
Free + subscriptionA basic counter; key features lockedA monthly or yearly fee that keeps charging until you cancel
Free + one-time unlockA working trackerOne payment if you want the extras, then nothing
Public or nonprofit toolsEverythingNothing, but they're often websites or printables, not live trackers

How to check an app before you download it

  1. Look for the in-app purchase note. Both stores show "In-App Purchases" on the listing, and Google Play also shows "Contains ads" when an app has them.
  2. Scroll to the price list. The App Store lists in-app purchase prices. Words like "monthly", "yearly" or "/mo" mean a subscription.
  3. Read the privacy label. App Privacy (App Store) and Data safety (Google Play) list what the app collects and whether it's linked to you.
  4. Check what's locked. Recent reviews often say which features need a payment, such as more than one habit or your full history.

Free alcohol tracker options

A sobriety tracker counts days since you stopped. A drink tracker logs how much you drink, for people cutting down. Both are available free:

  • NIAAA Rethinking Drinking (U.S. National Institute on Alcohol Abuse and Alcoholism): free calculators for drink size, calories and alcohol spending, plus worksheets for planning a change. See the tools page.
  • A spreadsheet. One column for the date, one for drinks. It's free and private.
  • Steady. Free for tracking days without alcohol, with check-ins and a journal for cravings.

Free support that isn't an app

  • SAMHSA National Helpline: 1-800-662-4357. Free, confidential, 24/7 treatment referral and information in the US.
  • 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline: call or text 988 in the US.
  • Mutual-help meetings such as Alcoholics Anonymous and SMART Recovery hold free meetings, in person and online.

Common questions

Is there a completely free sobriety tracker?

Yes. Steady's free version tracks one habit with a live clock, check-ins, money saved, milestones, a journal and a toolkit, with no ads and no time limit. A spreadsheet with =TODAY()-date also works for free.

Are free sobriety apps safe for my privacy?

It depends on the app. Read the App Privacy or Data safety label before downloading. Steady has no account and no server, so it doesn't collect what you log.

What are some free alcohol tracker apps and tools?

NIAAA's Rethinking Drinking site has free calculators and worksheets. For counting sober days, Steady is free to start. A simple spreadsheet works for logging drinks.

Why do some sobriety apps charge a subscription?

Apps that run servers, accounts or community features have ongoing costs. Steady stores everything on your phone, so it charges one optional payment instead of a monthly fee.
