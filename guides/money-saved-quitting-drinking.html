How Much Money Do You Save by Quitting Drinking?

By the Steady team · Published November 27, 2026 · Updated November 27, 2026

Short answer: Multiply what you spend on alcohol in a typical week by 52. At $10 a day you'd save about $304 a month and $3,650 a year. At $50 a week it's about $2,600 a year. Use the calculator below for your own number, then track it as it grows.

How to estimate what you used to spend

Most people underestimate this, so look at real numbers rather than guessing:

  • Bank and card statements for the last one to three months. Search for liquor stores, bars, delivery apps and the stores where you bought alcohol.
  • Bar and restaurant tabs, including tips and rounds for others.
  • The extras: rides home, late-night food, and anything you bought because you were drinking.

Divide the total by the number of days or weeks it covers. Even a rough daily figure is enough to see how fast the savings add up. NIAAA's free alcohol spending calculator can help you work it out drink by drink.

Make the savings real

Money you don't spend can quietly disappear into everyday costs. Some ways to keep it visible:

  • Move your daily or weekly amount into a separate savings account on payday.
  • Pick a goal for each milestone, such as something for 30 days or a trip at one year.
  • Pay down a debt with it and watch the balance fall.

Common questions

How much money do you save by quitting drinking?

Multiply your typical weekly spend by 52. Spending $10 a day means about $3,650 a year; $50 a week means $2,600 a year; $100 a week means $5,200 a year.

How do I calculate how much I spent on alcohol?

Add up alcohol purchases from one to three months of bank statements, including bars, stores, delivery and tips, then divide by the number of days covered to get a daily figure.

Is there an app that shows money saved from not drinking?

Yes. Steady shows money saved on its home screen once you add a daily cost, and it updates continuously. The feature is free.
