Is There an App That Can Track My Sober Time?

By the Steady team · Published November 27, 2026 · Updated November 27, 2026

Short answer: Yes. Sobriety tracker apps, also called sober counters or sobriety clocks, count the time since your sober date in days, hours and minutes. Most also mark milestones, show money saved and offer a daily check-in. Steady is one: it's free to start, needs no account and keeps everything on your phone.

What a sobriety tracker app does

A sobriety tracker counts up from the day you stopped drinking, using or doing a habit. The count is the core feature, but most apps add a few tools around it:

  • A live clock showing days, hours and minutes sober.
  • Milestone reminders at points such as 30, 90 and 365 days.
  • Money saved, worked out from what the habit used to cost per day.
  • Daily check-ins to mark each day you stayed on track.
  • A journal for moods, cravings and what set them off.
  • Help for hard moments, such as a breathing exercise, your reasons for quitting, or people and helplines to call.

What an app can't do

A tracker counts and reminds; it doesn't treat addiction. Many people use one alongside other support, such as a doctor, a counselor, a peer group or a helpline. In the US, the SAMHSA National Helpline (1-800-662-4357) is free, confidential and open 24 hours a day for treatment referrals and information.

Common questions

What is the best app to track sober time?

The best one is the one you'll open every day and trust with your data. Look for no required account, data kept on your phone, no ads, and a fair price. Steady meets all four and is free to start.

Can a sobriety app track things other than alcohol?

Yes. Steady tracks any habit you name, such as alcohol, nicotine, cannabis, gambling or sugar. With Premium you can track several at once, each with its own clock.

Does a sobriety tracker need the internet?

Steady doesn't. The clock, check-ins, journal and milestone notifications all run on your phone. Only the optional Premium backup uses the internet, to reach your own iCloud or Google Drive.

Do sobriety apps work?

A tracker is a tool, not a treatment. Many people find that seeing their day count and money saved keeps them motivated, and that logging cravings helps them spot patterns. It works best alongside other support.
